### 5.1 Measurement Fundamentals
- **Classical Test Theory:** Observed Score = True Score + Error. The observed number is never pure truth.
- **Reliability ceilings validity:** a score drowned in random error cannot be a trustworthy signal of any construct. High reliability does not prove validity.
- **Reliable ≠ valid:** a bootcamp exam with internal consistency 0.94 can still fail as a measure of job readiness — consistency does not say what is being measured.

### 5.2 Taxonomies of Learning
- **SOLO Prestructural** (Structure of the Observed Learning Outcome): the response misses the point and engages the task in an irrelevant or tautological way.
- **Bloom type problem:** Bloom handles how demanding a task is better than what *kind* of learning it is (the hierarchy problem is the other named critique).
- **Intellectual skill vs instance memory:** reusing the same debugging exercises on a final cannot separate debugging skill from memory of those instances.

### 5.3 Assessment Styles
- **Cheap auto-gradable formative risk:** frequent machine checks can quietly narrow the course toward whatever the software can grade.
- **Roediger and Karpicke / testing effect:** the advantage showed on a delayed test because durable retention, not immediate performance, is what matters.
- **Evidence-centered design (ECD):** settle the competency model (what you intend to measure) before the evidence model (what counts as observable evidence). Starting from cheap logs inverts the chain.

### 5.4 Grading Philosophies
- **Four jobs of a grade:** communicate, sort, motivate, and give feedback.
- **Contract grading:** most distinct because it changes who decides the grade and what the grade is based on (not just how a bar is written).
- **Ungrading:** when the mark itself is the problem (safe projects, ignoring critique text), replace the reported number with feedback and a closing conversation.

### 5.5 Feedback Frameworks
- **Guild knowledge** (Sadler): the expert’s often-tacit, hard-to-articulate sense of what good work looks like — it does not travel automatically to the student.
- **Why feedback bounces:** it assumes a shared standard of quality that teacher and student do not actually share.
- **Peer review without a target:** show reviewers the goal the work is aiming at before they write, or reviews feel arbitrary.

### 5.6 Alignment and Rubric Design
- **Analytic rubric:** break the work into separate criteria and score each independently.
- **Rubric grain changes the construct:** awarding points for naming each of three causes tells students that three are wanted and removes the need to recognize scope — different from scoring naming-plus-description.
- **Show rubrics course by course:** reject a blanket “publish every rubric with every syllabus” rule; set visibility/grain according to the construct assessed.

### 5.7 Peer Assessment
- **Averaging fails when errors correlate:** peer-rater errors are often not independent, so more raters do not cancel the bias.
- **Reliability vs fairness:** reliability asks whether peer grades are consistent; fairness asks whether they are consistent for reasons that have nothing to do with the work.
- **Meta-reviewer design:** graders evaluate the submission alongside the peer reviews it already got — improves grader feedback without making raw peer marks the final grade.

### 5.8 Ethical Considerations in Researching Learners
- **Undue influence:** the subtler pressure of not wanting to disappoint the person who controls your grade — not only an explicit threat of academic penalty.
- **Privacy over time:** research data often persists, gets shared, and gets re-analyzed for years in ways nobody foresaw — harder than a single collection act.
- **Stewardship / consent reuse:** one consent for a hint study does not license training a dropout model years later; revisit the use.

### 5.9 Ethical Considerations in Assessment
- **Bias at scale:** a biased assessment deployed widely harms an entire population in the same direction at once.
- **Why scale intensifies fairness (especially edtech):** a tool that reaches many learners visits the same bias on all of them at once.
- **Capture only what the assessment needs:** keystroke, mouse, and dwell traces “for someday” still owe learners a privacy interest even with no IRB study running.
