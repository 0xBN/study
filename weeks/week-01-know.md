### Course shape

- **Plain:** The class has three layers: the tool, the class around the tool, and how you measure whether it worked.
- **Quiz voice:** learning *technologies* → learning *experiences* → *measuring* learning.
- **Trap:** **Assessment** = this student’s grade or feedback. **Evaluation** = improve the tool or curriculum for the next cohort. Do not swap them.

### Mayer / load (named now; full Mayer is Module 3)

- **Plain:** Extra stuff on screen that does no work can make learning worse. A talking head next to slides usually does not help.
- **Quiz voice:** **Image Principle** (instructor face on video usually does not raise learning). **Coherence Principle** (meaningless extras can *hurt*). **Cognitive load** (Sweller): inherent difficulty / messy presentation / work of understanding. Split attention (face + slides + voice) is a tax unless both streams are needed.

### Constructivism (the -tiv word)

- **Plain:** You do not download knowledge. You rebuild a picture in your head. If the picture is wrong, telling you the right sentence is not enough.
- **Quiz voice:** Knowledge is a **mental model**. Piaget: **assimilation** (fit new stuff into the old picture), **accommodation** (change the picture), **disequilibrium** (the uncomfortable mismatch). Posner conceptual change: dissatisfied → intelligible → plausible → more useful. Vygotsky: social first; **zone of proximal development (ZPD)** = what you can do with help but not yet alone; the tool can be the “more-knowledgeable other.” **Scaffolding must fade.** Bjork **desirable difficulties**: hard in the *content*, not the UI. Kirschner, Sweller, Clark 2006: unguided discovery is **not** constructivism-as-theory; novices need guidance. Kapur **productive failure**: struggle, then a clear wrap-up.
- **Trap:** Unguided “just explore” is not the same claim as constructivism.

### Constructionism (the -tion word)

- **Plain:** You understand by *making a thing* other people can see — a program, a robot, a model — not only by thinking privately.
- **Quiz voice:** Papert: think *by* building; **objects-to-think-with**; hard fun. Resnick: **low floor** (easy to start), **high ceiling** (room to grow), **wide walls** (many kinds of project). **Four P’s** = **Projects, Passion, Peers, Play**.
- **Trap:** **Constructivism** (-tiv) = mental model. **Constructionism** (-tion) = that, plus a public artifact. They are not the same word.

### Direct instruction

- **Plain:** The teacher shows the move, you try it together, then you try it alone. That is teaching, not “I lecture, you figure it out.”
- **Quiz voice:** Explicit, structured teaching of a concept or skill. Includes modeling and feedback, not only talking. **Gradual release of responsibility** (Pearson and Gallagher, 1980s): **I do / we do / you do**. Same arc as fading scaffolds. Bad di is “I tell, you do.” Good di is compatible with constructivism. **Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006):** unguided discovery overloads novice working memory; “the advantage of guidance begins to recede only when learners have sufficiently high prior knowledge.” **Worked example effect** (Sweller and Cooper): novices often learn more from fully worked examples than from solving equivalents. **Expertise reversal** (Kalyuga): that advantage shrinks and flips as expertise grows. **lowercase di** = teacher-led teaching in general. **Capital-D Direct Instruction** = Engelmann and Becker (1960s): scripted, tiny steps, small groups, choral responses, field-tested. **Faultless communication:** if the student has not learned, the teaching was flawed. **Project Follow Through** (late 1960s–70s, eval 1977): DI at/near the top on basic skills, cognitive-conceptual skills, *and* affective measures (self-esteem) — not only drill. Hattie *Visible Learning*; Stockard et al. 2018 meta-analysis. **Rosenshine (2012) Principles of Instruction:** review prior learning; small steps + practice; lots of questions to *all* students; models/worked examples; guided then thinned practice; ~**80%** success in guided practice; weekly/monthly review (spacing). ITS / mastery systems = gradual release automated. Minecraft sandbox is *not* di; LEGO booklet *is*.
- **Trap:** Direct instruction ≠ lecturing. **Capital-D ≠ lowercase di.** Sage-on-the-stage (Alison King 1993) was coined as *criticism*. DI vs discovery is a false dichotomy; the variable is learner expertise.

### Behaviorism

- **Plain:** Ignore the inner movie. Learning is “did the behavior change?” Reward makes a behavior more likely; punishment makes it less likely. “Negative” here means *take something away*, not “mean.”
- **Quiz voice:** Learning = observable behavior change; mind is a black box. Reaction against **introspection**. Watson 1913 manifesto: if you cannot observe it, it is not science. Findings were not *wrong*; they were not everything. Pavlov **classical conditioning** (involuntary): **unconditioned stimulus (US)** / **unconditioned response (UR)** / **conditioned stimulus (CS)** / **conditioned response (CR)**. Dynamics: **acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization**. Little Albert (Watson and Rayner 1920). Test anxiety = test paired with stress. **Rescorla–Wagner:** not mere pairing; **prediction error** (surprise). Thorndike **Law of Effect** (1911, puzzle boxes): satisfying consequences more likely; unpleasant less likely — *voluntary* action, not reflexes. Skinner **operant conditioning**. **Shaping / successive approximations.** **Reinforcement increases** behavior; **punishment decreases**. **Positive = add; negative = remove.** Positive reinforcement add good; negative reinforcement remove bad (e.g. disable a time limit after chores). Positive punishment add bad; negative punishment remove good. **Negative reinforcement is not punishment.** Skinner: reinforcement generally better / less collateral damage than punishment. **Schedules:** fixed-ratio, fixed-interval, variable-interval, **variable-ratio** (most persistent; slots, likes). **Programmed instruction / teaching machines** (Skinner 1968 *The Technology of Teaching*): small **frames**, active response, immediate feedback, high success. Line to PLATO and ITS. **Learned helplessness** (Seligman and Maier 1967): actions seem disconnected from outcomes; later revision: helplessness is default, what is learned is expectation of *control*. Schultz: dopamine = **reward prediction error**.
- **Trap:** Negative reinforcement ≠ punishment. Classical (involuntary pairing) ≠ operant (voluntary consequences). Thorndike’s old “connectionism” ≠ modern neural nets.

### Cognitivism

- **Plain:** Now we do care what is inside: noticing, storing, pulling it back out. Memory is limited, so experts pack many pieces into one “chunk.”
- **Quiz voice:** Open the black box *without* returning to armchair introspection: behavior is a window into mind. **Tolman (1948):** **latent learning** / **cognitive maps** — layout learned with no reward. **Chomsky (1959)** review of Skinner *Verbal Behavior*: language too generative; **poverty of the stimulus**; innate structure. Mind as **information-processing**. MIT 1956 symposium (Miller, Chomsky, Newell and Simon). Ebbinghaus: **forgetting curve** (fast then levels); **spacing effect** (distributed > crammed) — a **desirable difficulty**. Atkinson–Shiffrin **multi-store**: sensory / short-term / long-term; attend + rehearse or lose it. Neisser 1967 named the field; later ecological cognition. Miller 1956 **7±2**; deeper point is **chunking**; Cowan 2001 ~**4** chunks. Expertise *is* chunking (chess). **Baddeley and Hitch working memory:** central executive + **phonological loop** (verbal) + **visuospatial sketchpad** — why narration + diagram can widen the bottleneck; verbatim on-screen text *plus* read-aloud jams one channel. Newell and Simon: **problem space** (states, operators, goals); **means-ends analysis**; **protocol analysis** (think-aloud *during* the task). **Cognitive load theory** (Sweller, late 1980s): **intrinsic** = inherent difficulty (element interactivity); **extraneous** = clutter / bad UI / anxiety tax; **germane** = productive schema-building (later formulations fold germane into intrinsic). Manage intrinsic by sequencing; cut extraneous; free capacity for germane. Learning = change in **knowledge / mental structure**, not only behavior. Constructivism = how people learn by building; cognitivism = how we *investigate* that thinking.
- **Trap:** Cognitivism is not a return to Freud-style introspection. Cramming *feels* effective because of the steep early forgetting curve.

### Human vs machine learning

- **Plain:** Psychology started with “watch behavior,” then opened the mind. AI started with hand-written rules, then learned from reward and data. Same road, opposite directions.
- **Quiz voice:** Learning science: behaviorism → cognitivism. AI: hand-coded symbols → train from experience (virtual Skinner boxes). **Physical Symbol System Hypothesis** (Newell and Simon 1976): symbol manipulation necessary and sufficient for general intelligence. **GOFAI / expert systems** (DENDRAL, MYCIN): brittle if–then libraries, no modern “learning.” **Connectionism:** Rosenblatt perceptron 1958; Minsky and Papert *Perceptrons* 1969 helped a winter; **backpropagation** 1986 (Rumelhart, Hinton, Williams); **AlexNet** 2012. **RLHF** = shaping via successive approximations. Sutton **temporal difference**; Sutton and Barto textbook. Schultz, Dayan, Montague 1997: dopamine ≈ TD **reward prediction error**. Yamins/DiCarlo: convnets predict primate visual cortex. Language models: unpredictable words → slower reading / surprise; Schrimpf/Fedorenko 2021. Analogy itself is an engine of discovery (computer-as-mind, then nets-as-brain).
- **Trap:** Modern nets are *not* expert-system rule dumps. Behaviorism’s “ignore inner experience” fits AI better than it ever fit people.

### Metacognition

- **Plain:** Thinking about your own thinking: “Is this working?” then “I’ll try another way.” It uses the same scarce attention as the problem itself, so panic eats it. Teach it until it is as cheap as walking.
- **Quiz voice:** Monitor what you know, notice gaps, plan next. **Nelson and Narens (1990):** **object level** (the work) vs **meta level**. **Monitoring** flows up; **control** flows down. **Flavell (1979):** **metacognitive knowledge** about **persons, tasks, strategies** + **metacognitive experiences** (in-the-moment confusion/confidence). **Ann Brown:** **regulation** — plan, monitor, evaluate; with Palincsar, **reciprocal teaching** (1984): predict, question, clarify, summarize. High achievers distinguished by metacognition, not only raw ability. **Self-regulated learning (SRL)** (Zimmerman): **forethought → performance → self-reflection**. Digital settings raise the stakes (Kizilcec MOOCs 2017). Dashboards / Azevedo **MetaTutor** = externalize the loop. **Calibration** / judgments of learning: people overestimate; **fluency illusion** (rereading feels like knowing). **Dunning–Kruger:** least skilled most overconfident. Bjork: what *feels* like learning is often the opposite of what works. Practice quizzes **recalibrate**. **Transfer:** apply learning in a new setting (analogy). Thorndike and Woodworth **identical elements**. **Near vs far** transfer. Salomon and Perkins: **low-road** (automatic in similar context) vs **high-road** (deliberate abstraction). Analogies = pre-packaged high-road transfer. **Help-seeking chain:** notice confusion → decide if productive → choose source → evaluate. **Adaptive** vs **executive/over-reliant** vs **avoidant**. Baker **gaming the system**; Aleven/Koedinger **Help Tutor**. Graduated hints; protect productive struggle.
- **Trap:** Fluency ≠ mastery. Help that dumps the answer is executive help-seeking, not adaptive.

### Sociocultural

- **Plain:** A lot of “smart” is borrowed from other people and tools first, then it becomes yours. The classroom/culture is not wallpaper; it is the source.
- **Quiz voice:** Learning through participation in cultural practices. Vygotsky **general genetic law:** every higher function appears **twice** — **interpsychological** (between people) then **intrapsychological** (inside). ZPD is the mechanism of that move inward. **Mediation:** never meet the world raw; **technical tools** (hammer) change the world; **psychological tools** (numbers, maps, language, diagrams) change thinking. Language path: social speech → **private speech** → **inner speech** (Piaget called self-talk egocentric and fading; Vygotsky: it goes underground). **Internalization.** Luria: schooled people group hammer/saw/axe as *tools* (taxonomic); unschooled group saw+log (functional activity). Cole **cultural psychology**: culture woven into cognition, not layered on. Rogoff **guided participation** / **learning by observing and pitching in**. **Activity theory** (Leont’ev): unit = whole **activity**. **Engeström activity system:** subject, object, tools + community, rules, division of labor. Reich *Failure to Disrupt*: tech fails when it ignores that system. **Communities of practice** (Lave and Wenger 1991): **legitimate peripheral participation**. **Distributed cognition** (Hutchins *Cognition in the Wild*): ship navigation is the *system* thinking. **Cognitive apprenticeship** (Collins, Brown, Newman 1989): model, coach, scaffold, articulate, reflect, explore.
- **Trap:** ZPD (the zone) ≠ scaffolding (the support). Piaget = individual exploration; Vygotsky = social first. Sit-still-and-listen school is *a* cultural practice, not the only one.

### Motivation

- **Plain:** Not an on/off switch. Direction + intensity. Same levers can help or manipulate. There is no single right theory — tools for different students.
- **Quiz voice:** **Self-determination theory** (Deci and Ryan): **autonomy, competence, relatedness** → **intrinsic** motivation. Continuum of extrinsic: **external regulation** (pure reward/punishment) → **introjected** (guilt/ego) → **identified** (personally values outcome) → **integrated** (who I am). Aim for high-quality extrinsic when intrinsic is unavailable. Deci 1971: paying people to do puzzles *cut* later free play. **Expectancy–value** (Eccles, Wigfield, Atkinson): **expectancy** (can I succeed?) × **value** (do I care?) — **multiplicative**, either near zero kills motivation. Value splits: **attainment, intrinsic, utility**; minus **cost** (time/energy/opportunity — why AI-copying happens online). Diagnose *which* is missing. **Goal orientation** (Nicholls, Dweck, Ames; Elliot 2×2): **mastery vs performance** × **approach vs avoidance**. Mottos: “learn as much as possible” / “avoid misunderstanding” / “show what I know” / “avoid looking dumb.” Research favors mastery + approach for deep learning. **Dweck mindset:** **fixed** vs **growth**; growth → mastery goals; fixed → performance-avoidance. Mindset is a simplified schema of goal orientation. **Attribution** (Weiner): **locus** (internal/external) × **stability** × **controllability**. Healthiest failure story: **controllable** causes (effort, strategy). **Attributional retraining.** Feedback after a wrong answer *is* a motivational intervention. **Flow** (Csíkszentmihályi): challenge matched to skill + clear goals + immediate feedback; channel between anxiety and boredom; **autotelic**. Walton and Cohen **belonging**. Task-focused feedback (“strengthen this argument”) vs person-focused (“you’re a strong writer” = fixed). Hidi and Renninger **interest:** triggered situational → maintained → emerging individual → well-developed. **Keller ARCS:** Attention, Relevance, Confidence, Satisfaction. **Dark patterns:** **overjustification** (Lepper, Greene, Nisbett 1973 — reward kids for drawing → less free drawing); **reward saturation**; **shallow gamification**; leaderboards help performance-approach, hurt performance-avoidance.
- **Trap:** Motivation is not binary. Leaderboards are not “always motivating.” External rewards can kill intrinsic interest.

### Ethics

- **Plain:** Students often cannot leave your tool (Canvas, Honorlock). You must not harm them, and you must not lock out the people who need access.
- **Quiz voice:** Two duties: **prevent harm** (they did not choose you) vs **ensure access** (they have no choice but to use you). Reich: **toxic power of data** vs EdTech **Matthew effect** (already-advantaged get more). Privacy: students rarely consent; **FERPA, COPPA** (under 13), **GDPR** are a floor. **inBloom** (~2013) died on trust, not tech. **Privacy** = how data is used *after* collection; **oversurveillance** = whether to collect. Watched students take fewer intellectual risks. **Data minimization:** only what you will use to help learning. **Lifecycle stewardship:** expire, anonymize, delete; not a permanent behavior rap sheet. Algorithmic bias becomes the learning experience (Noble, O’Neil; remote-proctoring darker-skin false flags). **Dark patterns** (Brignull): juice engagement metrics without learning. **Learning styles** (visual/auditory/kinesthetic) are a **myth** — Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, Bjork 2008; Kirschner 2016; meshing hypothesis failed; any benefit is placebo / extra teacher care. Real differences: prior knowledge, language, access, disability. Accessibility is legal **minimum** (ADA, Section 508, **WCAG**); aim at the *learning experience*, not only the UI. **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** (CAST; Rose and Meyer) — full treatment Module 3. **Curb-cut effect:** captions help more than the originally intended group. Inclusion = see yourself in examples/agents (Baylor). Be *deliberate* about who an analogy includes. Also: student IP into AI training; screen addiction; transparency.
- **Trap:** Learning styles ≠ learner differences. Accessibility checklist ≠ accessible *learning*. Captions-only ≠ equivalent experience to audio.

### CRUM (Computational-Representational Understanding of Mind)

- **Plain:** Thinking is “structures that stand for things” plus “steps that run on those structures.” Like data plus algorithms, for the mind. Not only logic — neural-style nets count too.
- **Quiz voice:** Representational structures + computational procedures. Connectionism still counts. **Multifarious** (no one type is enough), **successful**, **incomplete** (needs brains + social — his Part II). Six types, in order: logic, rules, concepts, analogies, images, connections.

### Explanation schema (every chapter)

- **Plain:** To explain a smart behavior: people have representations of type X, processes Y on them, and Y applied to X produces the behavior.

### Five criteria (how to grade an approach)

- **Plain:** Does it have enough kinds of idea? Can it plan, decide, explain, learn, do language? Does it match psychology? Brains? Can you actually use it?
- **Quiz voice:** representational power; computational power; psychological plausibility; neurological plausibility; practical applicability.

### Mind–computer–brain

- **Plain:** A program is data structures + algorithms. A mind, in this course, is representations + procedures. The third comparison is the brain (especially connectionism). Rough analogy — not “the brain is a PC.” Serial vs parallel.

### Marr’s three levels (lecture, not Thagard Ch. 1)

- **Plain:** You can ask (1) what problem is being solved, (2) what recipe and data, (3) what hardware. Do not answer “what is the problem?” with “neurotransmitters.”
- **Quiz voice:** **Computational** = what problem. **Algorithmic** = algorithms + data structures. **Implementation** = neurons / silicon. Thagard’s own map is CRUM + six types + five criteria, not Marr.

### Logic (Ch. 2)

- **Plain:** Ideas as sentences you can prove from. “If A then B; A; so B.” You can also guess a hidden cause to *explain* a clue (Holmes), or generalize from examples.
- **Quiz voice:** Predicate-logic-like sentences; **deductive and inductive** procedures. **Modus ponens** (if A then B; A; therefore B). **Abduction** (Peirce): hypothesize in order to *explain* (Frank didn’t show → maybe sick). **Induction:** narrow = generalize from instances; broad = any uncertain inference (abduction counts). **Bayes** for P(hypothesis | evidence). Thagard: logic is a strong *tool*; he is not sure it is the *core* of human thought. Lecture: Holmes clip = abduction.

### Probability / Monty Hall (lecture)

- **Plain:** If we used the math we would switch doors (~2/3). Most people stay. So the mind may not “run probability,” even though AI does.
- **Quiz voice:** **Bounded rationality** (Simon): incomplete info + limited compute → **satisficing** (good enough, not perfect).

### Rules (Ch. 3 + lecture)

- **Plain:** Lots of “if this, then that,” plus hunting through possible moves, plus making *new* if-thens. Looks like logic’s if-then; it is not the same as proving modus ponens.
- **Quiz voice:** Mental rules + **search** + procedures that **form new rules**. Newell/Simon; Anderson **ACT**; **SOAR**. **Domain/world** knowledge vs **control/strategy** (control is often unspoken). **Heuristics** = rules of thumb (availability heuristic). **Power law of practice** (Thagard).

### Concepts (Ch. 4 + lecture)

- **Plain:** A concept is not a perfect dictionary definition. It is a bundle of typical features. This red light in front of you is a **percept**; **red-as-stop** is the concept.
- **Quiz voice:** Drop **classical** strict definitions. Typical features; **approximate match**. **Schemas/scripts** = bigger bundles. Slots, kind/part hierarchies; spreading activation, matching, **inheritance**. Quiz-bait types: **axiomatic** / **prototypical** / **exemplar** / **qualia**.
