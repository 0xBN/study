### 3.1 TPACK (Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge)
- **TPACK** (Mishra and Koehler): extends Shulman by adding Technological Knowledge (TK) as a third foundational domain beside content and pedagogy. Desmos for functions is not just a faster chalkboard — the tool changes what you can show and how you teach.
- **Technological content knowledge (TCK):** how a tool reshapes what parts of the content are easy to see and ask about. Prefer a plate-tectonics simulation over a narrated video when the goal is to foreground boundaries forming under learner control.
- **TPACK as design capacity** (Angeli and Valanides): when you build a tool you are making TPACK decisions for teachers and learners. A grammar app that only does fill-in-the-blank has already chosen a pedagogy.

### 3.2 Project- and Problem-Based Learning
- **Challenging driving question** (Joyner’s own cut): what matters is whether there is a single right answer to judge against. True challenging questions have better/worse answers, not one correct key.
- **Collaboration as core** (Vygotsky framing in the lesson): peers supply support inside the zone of proximal development (ZPD) — what you can do with help but not alone. Groups are not just a way to split labor.
- **Assess the project work:** if students spend weeks making documentaries, treat the documentaries and drafts as the evidence of learning, not a later multiple-choice test about the same unit.

### 3.3 Curriculum
- **Backward design** (Wiggins and McTighe, *Understanding by Design*, 1998): design from desired results and acceptable evidence backward into activities. Start with what learners should be able to do, then build the path.
- **This course’s sequence trade-off** (Joyner): covering assessment last is the “wrong” order for backward design, accepted as a more motivating sequence instead of a slightly more effective one.
- **Curriculum ≠ topic list:** a list of thirty topics with time boxes is inventory, not curriculum. Scope, sequence, depth, and path decisions are missing.

### 3.4 Mayer's Multimedia Learning Principles
- **Spatial contiguity** (Mayer): corresponding words and images should sit near each other on the page or screen, not separated into a distant legend.
- **Modality** (Mayer): graphics plus spoken narration beat graphics plus a long on-screen paragraph when both compete at once. Move the explanation into narration while an animated chart builds.
- **First vs second cluster** (Joyner’s grouping): first cluster is ruthless cutting/reorganizing so working memory is not wasted; second cluster paces and sequences genuinely hard material (pre-training, segmenting, and related moves).

### 3.5 Accessibility and Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
- **UDL origin** (Rose and Meyer at CAST): adapted Universal Design from architecture (Ron Mace) into education so experiences work for varied learners from the start.
- **UDL vs learning styles (most important difference):** provide multiple representations to everyone; do not sort each learner into a matched channel.
- **Curb-cut effect:** captions and similar access features help far more people than the enrollment survey’s “hearing impairment” count — same pattern as sidewalk curb cuts helping strollers and carts.

### 3.6 Hyflex and the Distributed Classroom Matrix
- **Space spectrum** (Joyner and Isbell): with the instructor; with a cohort but remote from the instructor; or fully remote alone.
- **Invest in distributed materials:** content can outlive the class and reach audiences you did not plan for, so quality is worth the cost.
- **Certificate vs credit:** without verifying who did the work, you can offer a certificate for structured completion with some accountability — not academic credit.

### 3.7 The SAMR Model (Substitution, Augmentation, Modification, Redefinition)
- **Augmentation** (Puentedura; writing example): same essay task, but spell check, easy reorganize, and digital submit add functional improvement beyond Substitution typing.
- **SAMR vs TPACK:** SAMR is a quick read of how much the tech changes the task; TPACK judges whether going further is pedagogically wise. Complementary, not competing.
- **Right level = learning goal:** holding software at “student chooses and defends the test” while the tool could auto-pick is deliberate Enhancement-level fit, not a failure to climb to Modification.

### 3.8 Communities of Inquiry (CoI)
- **Integration phase** (Practical Inquiry Model; Garrison, Anderson, Archer): make sense of ideas from exploration and build connections into coherent understanding. Hard alone; community matters most here.
- **Social presence → cognitive presence:** weak social presence blocks honest discourse, so genuine integration (cognitive presence) is harder.
- **Facilitating discourse** (teaching presence): when many good posts sit unconnected, name the threads that connect and ask the group to reconcile them — do not just dump the “correct” answer.

### 3.9 Ethical Considerations in Learning Experience Design
- **AI authorship (for now):** most defensible stance is transparency — what was generated, by what means, and what role human judgment played.
- **Sociopolitical consciousness** (Ladson-Billings): often the hardest of academic success / cultural competence / sociopolitical consciousness, because it asks students to critique systems they are also trying to succeed within.
- **Inclusive assessment:** if the goal is reasoning, not presentation skill, offer multiple means of demonstrating mastery — do not treat one timed oral format as the construct.
